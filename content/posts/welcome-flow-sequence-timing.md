A welcome flow is the automated series of emails a new subscriber receives after joining your list. Build it as three to five emails over one to two weeks: introduce the brand, show your best products, answer the doubts that stop people buying, and make a first-order offer if it suits the brand.

It is one of the first flows we build for any account, because a new subscriber is at their most interested the day they sign up.

## Why the welcome flow matters

Someone who just gave you their email address is paying more attention to you than they ever will again. A single welcome email wastes that. A short sequence uses it.

If you want the definition on its own, see [what a welcome flow is](/glossary/welcome-flow).

## A structure that works

The exact number and timing should be tested for your store, but this is a sound starting point.

**Email 1: sent immediately.** Welcome them, say who you are in a sentence, deliver whatever you promised at sign-up (a code, a guide), and give one clear next step. Sending it straight away matters, because intent fades fast.

**Email 2: a day later.** Show your brand story or what makes you different. For a fashion or premium brand, lead with your strongest imagery. Do not lead with a discount before the brand has said anything.

**Email 3: two to three days later.** Show best sellers or a guide to choosing. Answer the questions that stop people buying: shipping, returns, sizing, how it works.

**Email 4: around day five to seven.** Add proof. Reviews, press or customer photos earn trust that copy cannot.

**Email 5: around day ten, optional.** A reminder of the offer with an honest reason to act now, such as a code with a real expiry.

## Timing notes

- Send the first email immediately, not on a schedule.
- Space the rest more widely over time as interest cools.
- Add a filter so anyone who purchases leaves the flow and moves to a post-purchase flow instead.
- Respect time zones if your audience is spread across regions.

## What to say

Keep each email focused on one idea and one button. A welcome email that tries to say everything says nothing.

Write in the voice of the brand, not in marketing boilerplate. If the brand story is strong, tell it. If the product needs education, teach. For a considered, higher-ticket product, a longer, slower sequence usually works better than pressure.

## Offers: use them carefully

A first-order incentive can help, but it is not mandatory and it trains customers to wait for discounts. Premium brands often do better leading with craft and proof. If you do offer a code, a real expiry gives the first purchase a reason to happen this week.

## Common mistakes

- Opening with a discount before the brand has said anything.
- Sending only one email.
- No exit once the person has bought.
- Long, image-only emails that fail when images are blocked.
- Never testing subject lines, send times or the order of the emails.

## How to know if it works

Measure the flow by attributed revenue and revenue per recipient, not opens. Compare each email to see which ones earn and which ones lose people, then change the weakest one first.
